The following questions are drafted for submission to the official organizer Q&A, based directly on the items in `derived/qa-pack-v10/QUESTIONS.md`.

---

### Question 1: Minimum robot footprint and expansion retention (QA01)
Rule GM1 V3 R01 (line 242) states that the robot must have a minimum size of 10 inches long by 10 inches wide for 3 inches up from the surface. Does a circular chassis with a 10-inch diameter satisfy this minimum dimensional requirement? Additionally, GM1 V2 R01 (line 234) prohibited contracting this portion at any time, but that restriction was omitted in GM1 V3; must the lower 10-by-10-inch volume remain maintained for the entire duration of the match?  
**What we need answered:** Whether a 10-inch diameter round chassis is permissible under R01, and whether the lower chassis is permitted to contract after match start.

---

### Question 2: AprilTag placement and pre-match Technician adjustments (QA02)
Section 3.3 (line 170) states that the pit assignment AprilTag is mounted to the wall separating the pit area from the racecourse, whereas §3.4.1 (line 174) and Appendix G state it is fastened to the wall by the starting area. Could you clarify the exact mounting location, height, and facing of the AprilTag, and whether it will be directly visible from inside the start box? Furthermore, §3.4.1 (line 179) notes that the Technician declares settings selected before match start, but GS01 (line 212) forbids robot interaction after field element randomization; is a team permitted to manually set a switch on the robot after the pit box tag is revealed without forfeiting task points?  
**What we need answered:** The exact physical location and orientation of the AprilTag, and whether manual robot configuration is permitted after field randomization.

---

### Question 3: Obstacle vehicle specifications and quadrant placement (QA03)
Section 3.1 (line 127) and Appendix B (line 321) list three randomly placed obstacle vehicles from a "Toy Vehicle Obstacle Set" placed in quadrants on the straightaways (§3.4.1, line 175). What are the specific model names and outer footprint dimensions of these toy vehicles? Must each obstacle vehicle rest entirely inside its 16-by-16-inch quadrant, can they be oriented at arbitrary angles, and can two obstacle vehicles be assigned to the same straightaway? Lastly, Appendix E (line 379) defines quadrants starting 2 inches from the side rail and stopping 2 inches from the center rail for a total width of 16 inches, but the track rails are approximately 22 inches apart; from which rail faces are these 2-inch offsets measured?  
**What we need answered:** The exact dimensions of the obstacle toys, their allowed placement orientations/boundaries within quadrants, and the reference faces used for Appendix E measurements.

---

### Question 4: Track markings and lane boundaries (QA04)
Section 3.2 (line 131) defines only two areas on the field: the racecourse and the pit area. Are robots permitted to drive over or cross the white lane markings painted on the racecourse surface? At the turns, the corridor between the white line and the outer wall is approximately 8.4 to 8.7 inches wide, which is narrower than the 10-inch minimum robot width in GM1 V3 R01 (line 242). Beyond physical walls and dividers, do painted markings impose any lane restrictions or penalties?  
**What we need answered:** Whether painted white lines restrict robot paths or impose lane boundaries, or if only physical walls govern legal movement.

---

### Question 5: Pit entry and exit openings (QA05)
Appendix G (lines 390 and 393) requires the robot to enter the pit area during the pit stop, exchange tires, and enter the assigned pit box. Which of the checkered openings in the dividing wall may a robot use to enter and exit the pit area? Is the robot allowed to enter and exit using the same opening, and is it permissible to traverse through the starting area while navigating into or out of the pit?  
**What we need answered:** Which checkered openings are approved for pit transit, whether the same opening can be used for both entry and exit, and whether passing through the start box is permitted.

---

### Question 6: Lap direction, lap counting, and victory lap timing (QA06)
Section 3.2.2 (line 149) requires completing three laps around the course in under 3 minutes, with a pit stop between laps 1 and 2 or laps 2 and 3 (line 150). Are robots required to run laps in a specific direction (clockwise vs. counter-clockwise), and is reversing allowed on course? Because pit access may require crossing the start/finish line extra times, how will the referee count completed laps? In addition, for the school flag bonus described in §3.1 (line 127), does the final victory lap officially begin when the robot exits the pit after lap 2, or upon its next crossing of the start/finish line?  
**What we need answered:** The required travel direction, the method used to count laps during pit entry/exit crossings, and the exact event that marks the start of the final victory lap.

---

### Question 7: Tire exchange method, pit stop requirement, and match timing (QA07)
Appendix G contains conflicting statements, noting first that "the robot itself does not have to enter the assigned pit box" (line 392), but immediately listing "enter assigned Pit Box" in its sequence (line 393). Must the robot's physical chassis cross into the pit box, or may it remain outside the box and manipulate the tires using an reaching mechanism? Also, is completing a pit stop mandatory in order to earn the 100 points for crossing the finish line within 3 minutes under §3.6 (line 222)? Finally, is the 75% parking threshold check (§3.3, line 160) and overall match elapsed time measured when the robot crosses the finish line or when it comes to a complete stop inside the pit box?  
**What we need answered:** Whether a robot must physically enter the pit box or can reach in, whether a pit stop is required to earn the finish-line completion points, and the exact moment when match time and parking completion are judged.

---

### Question 8: Start flag mechanism and Appendix F details (QA08)
Section 3.1 (line 127) states that "when the green flag is lowered, your Robot will begin," while §3.4.1 (line 176) and §3.4.2 (line 181) state that the flag begins secured in the down position and the referee raises the flag to start the match. Which physical flag motion (raising or lowering) signifies match start? Where is the flag assembly mounted on the field, and when will the mechanical specifications in Appendix F (line 383) be published?  
**What we need answered:** Whether the match starts on a flag raise or flag lower, where the start flag is mounted, and when Appendix F will be released.

---

### Question 9: Positional tolerance stacking and field perimeter construction (QA09)
Rule GM2 V3 G02 (line 202) provides a positional tolerance of +/- 1 inch for competition-provided field elements. Can this tolerance stack independently across adjacent elements, potentially narrowing race corridors or turn passages by up to 2 inches? Furthermore, are the perimeter and dividing walls secured inside the 4-by-8-foot surface footprint, and are the walls constructed from nominal 3/4-inch lumber or actual 1.0-inch material?
